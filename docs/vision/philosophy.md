# New Me – Product Vision & Philosophy

## Core Mission

New Me is not just a habit tracker.

It is a system that helps people intentionally become the person they want to become.

The app is based on a software engineering mindset: **requirements come before implementation.**

Just as engineers first define requirements before writing code, users should first define who they want to become before deciding which habits to build.

## Product Philosophy

Most habit trackers ask: *"What habits do you want to build?"*

New Me asks: *"Who do you want to become?"*

Habits are not the goal. Habits are simply one implementation that helps satisfy the requirements of becoming that person.

## Engineering Mindset Applied to Personal Growth

**Software Development:**

Vision → Requirements → Design → Implementation → Testing → Monitoring → Iteration

**New Me:**

Identity → Requirements → Systems → Habits → Reflection → Growth → Iteration

The user is constantly improving themselves the same way great software evolves.

## Two Main Modes

### 1. Simple Mode

Designed for users who want something fast and uncomplicated.

Features:
- Create habits
- Complete habits
- Streaks
- Reminders
- Basic statistics
- Clean UI

No philosophy is forced. The goal is to be one of the easiest habit trackers to use.

### 2. Growth Mode (Optional)

Unlocked over time or enabled manually. Focuses on long-term personal development rather than simply tracking habits.

Growth Mode introduces:
- Identity
- Purpose
- Reflection
- Requirements
- Systems Thinking
- Long-term progress

Users choose whether they want this experience.

## Identity Mode (Advanced)

Identity Mode should not appear immediately. Instead, the app waits until users demonstrate engagement and consistency.

For example:

> "You've been building habits consistently. Would you like to explore Identity Mode?"

Identity Mode helps you think beyond habits and focuses on the person you're becoming.

### Identity First

Instead of asking *"What habit should I build?"* — ask *"Who do I want to become?"*

Examples:
- Healthy father
- Better husband
- Great software engineer
- Disciplined student
- Strong athlete

### Requirements Gathering

Exactly like software engineering. Once identity is defined: *what requirements must this person satisfy?*

Example — Identity: **Become a dependable father**

Requirements:
- Healthy
- Financially responsible
- Patient
- Present
- Honest
- Reliable

These become the foundation.

### Systems Before Habits

Requirements are fulfilled by systems.

Example — Requirement: **Healthy**

Systems:
- Exercise
- Nutrition
- Sleep
- Recovery

Only then do habits appear, e.g.:
- Workout 4x/week
- Meal prep Sundays
- Sleep before 11 PM

### Habits Are Evidence

Instead of asking *"Did I complete my workout?"* — ask *"Am I becoming stronger?"*

Instead of *"Did I journal?"* — ask *"Am I becoming more self-aware?"*

Instead of *"Did I save money?"* — ask *"Am I becoming financially responsible?"*

The habit is not the goal. The transformation is.

### Reflection Instead of Checklists

Instead of only seeing ✅ Gym, users should also see:
- Strength ↑
- Energy ↑
- Confidence ↑
- Consistency ↑

Reflection focuses on outcomes rather than merely completion.

### Personalized Motivation

The user defines their own "why."

Examples:
- "I want to play with my future kids."
- "I want to become an engineer."
- "I want to stop procrastinating."
- "I want to be a dependable husband."

Reminders should reference the user's own words, e.g.:

> "You told yourself you wanted to become a dependable father. Today's workout supports that goal."

The app never invents motivation. It reflects the user's own purpose back to them.

### Growth Over Time

Requirements evolve. Goals evolve. Identity evolves.

The app should periodically ask: *do these goals still represent who you're trying to become?*

Life changes. The app grows with the user.

## Meet Past You

One of the long-term flagship features.

The app periodically stores snapshots of the user's life. Each snapshot may include:
- Goals
- Identity
- Reasons
- Journal
- Progress
- Pictures
- Confidence
- Health metrics
- Habits
- Reflections

Months or years later: **Meet Your Past Self.**

Users compare: Past Identity ↔ Current Identity ↔ Growth ↔ Reflection ↔ Pictures.

Instead of comparing themselves to other people, they compare themselves to who they used to be.

### Letters Across Time

One of the most emotionally meaningful features. Past You writes a message.

Example:

> "I know this is hard. I hope you didn't give up. I hope we became healthier. I hope we became disciplined."

Years later, Present You reads it. Then replies. Future versions of yourself continue the conversation.

The app becomes a journal of growth across time.

### Shareable Growth

Instead of sharing streaks, users share their journey:
- Before/after photos
- Personal growth timeline
- Annual reflection
- Lessons learned
- Goals achieved
- Letters from past self

The emphasis is not comparison. It is personal transformation.

## Guiding Principles

**Simplicity First** — The app should never overwhelm users. Simple Mode should remain incredibly easy. Advanced features are optional.

**Growth Is Earned** — Identity Mode unfolds gradually. Users discover deeper features naturally. The app grows as they grow.

**Reflection Beats Streaks** — Checking off habits is useful. Understanding personal growth is transformational.

**Compare Against Yourself** — Never encourage comparison against others. The only comparison that matters is Past Me → Present Me → Future Me.

**Build for Long-Term Change** — The app is designed around becoming someone. Not merely completing tasks.

**Solve a Real Problem** — Every feature should answer one question: *"What problem does this solve for the user?"* This stops feature creep. Before adding anything, ask:
- What user is this for?
- What problem does it solve?
- Can I make it simpler?
